1. WHAT ARE WE PREDICTING? (THE 3 TARGET OUTPUTS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Our system processes an incoming crime incident or surveillance scenario anywhere across all 28
Indian States & 8 Union Territories and simultaneously predicts THREE critical operational outputs:

[TARGET 1] CRIME HEAT LEVEL (Multi-Class Classification)
  - Target Variable: HeatLevel (High Heat / Medium Heat / Low Heat)
  - Model: XGBoost Multi-Class Classifier
  - Performance: 99.83% Accuracy | 1.0000 ROC-AUC
  - Operational Meaning:
      * HIGH HEAT (Level 3 - Critical Hotspot | CHI >= 60): Immediate tactical intervention / SWAT dispatch.
      * MEDIUM HEAT (Level 2 - Elevated Surveillance | 35 <= CHI < 60): Targeted mobile patrol route.
      * LOW HEAT (Level 1 - Routine Patrol | CHI < 35): Regular beat police monitoring.

[TARGET 2] VIOLENT CRIME THREAT PROBABILITY (Binary Probability Estimation)
  - Target Variable: IsViolentCrime (0 or 1, outputted as probability percentage 0% - 100%)
  - Model: Random Forest Classifier
  - Performance: 98.41% Accuracy | 0.9984 ROC-AUC
  - Operational Meaning:
      * Estimates the risk percentage that the incident will escalate into severe physical harm,
        assault, or lethal violence based on weapon lethality, time of day, and crime domain.

[TARGET 3] RECOMMENDED POLICE PATROL UNITS (Continuous Resource Optimization)
  - Target Variable: Police Deployed / Optimal Patrol Resource Count (1 - 12 units)
  - Model: Random Forest Regressor
  - Performance: 0.942 R² Score | Low RMSE
  - Operational Meaning:
      * Quantifies the exact number of tactical patrol vehicles and personnel needed to effectively
        suppress and neutralize the emerging hotspot.

2. INPUT FEATURES USED FOR PREDICTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When an incident is simulated or reported, the models ingest the following 13 key features:

  1. State                : All 28 States & 8 Union Territories of India (Encoded)
  2. City                 : 190+ Metropolitan & Tier-2/3 Indian Cities (Encoded)
  3. Crime Description    : IPC Crime Category (Homicide, Robbery, Burglary, Kidnapping, Assault, etc.)
  4. Weapon Used          : Firearm, Knife, Blunt Object, Explosive, Poison, None, etc.
  5. Hour of Incident     : 0 to 23 (24-hour diurnal scale)
  6. Day of Week          : Monday through Sunday (0 to 6)
  7. Is Weekend           : Boolean flag (Surge factor for weekend nightlife and transit)
  8. Month / Season       : Seasonal weather and festival activity patterns
  9. Time Of Day          : Night (22-04), Evening (17-21), Afternoon (12-16), Morning (05-11)
  10. Police Deployed     : Initial active police strength in the precinct (1 to 20)
  11. Victim Age          : Vulnerability demographic factor (0 to 99)
  12. Victim Gender       : Demographic vulnerability profile (M / F / Transgender)
  13. Micro-Coordinates   : Precised Latitude and Longitude with urban sector jitter

3. CRIME HEAT INDEX (CHI) FORMULATION & MATHEMATICAL DEFINITION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Crime Heat Index (CHI) is a normalized thermal metric on a 0 - 100 scale:

  CHI = SeverityWeight * WeaponHazard * TemporalRisk * (1 + 10 / (Police_Deployed + 5))

Where:
  - SeverityWeight : IPC gravity scale (0.35 for Property Theft -> 1.00 for Homicide/Kidnapping)
  - WeaponHazard   : Lethality factor (1.0 for None -> 1.5 for Knife -> 1.8 for Firearm)
  - TemporalRisk   : Diurnal curve peaking during late-night hours (22:00 - 03:00) and weekends (1.4x - 1.6x)
  - Police Deficit : (1 + 10 / (Police_Deployed + 5)) penalizes precincts with inadequate patrol density.

4. WHY WE CHOSE THESE SPECIFIC ML ALGORITHMS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) for Heat Level Classification:
   - Gradient boosted trees handle mixed continuous and categorical feature spaces without requiring strict linearity.
   - Built-in L1/L2 regularization prevents overfitting on localized regional spikes.
   - Fast inference speed (<2ms per prediction) enables real-time dispatch decision-making.

2. Random Forest Classifier for Violent Threat Probability:
   - Ensemble of decorrelated decision trees (bagging) produces well-calibrated class probabilities.
   - Robust against outliers and noisy reporting delays in crime data.

3. Random Forest Regressor for Patrol Resource Allocation:
   - Provides bounded, monotonic predictions for patrol unit counts without erratic extrapolations.
